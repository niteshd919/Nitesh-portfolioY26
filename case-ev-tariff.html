Tariff Based EV Charging

EV Tariff Input

A paper tariff, a phone, and not one uncovered minute.

01 · The short version

Every minute of the year needs a price.

200+ customers onboarded to tariff-based charging through the setup I designed. It gets a paper tariff into a smart wallbox, so the car charges when power is cheapest. About 18 values to type instead of 32, and no way to leave a minute out.

02 · What makes it hard

It looks like a form. It’s a transcription job.

People copy up to sixteen prices off a paper contract, and from/to fields leave every gap for them to catch.

11 · Outcomes

200+ customers onboarded. No minute goes without a price.

Built and shipped with a product manager and engineers. These are historical figures, not live data.

  • 200+ Customers onboarded to the feature
  • 60% Increase in user satisfaction
  • 90% Reduction in task complexity

The evidence: the review in the Figma file

“Showing exact price against each slot increased the user satisfaction by 60% and reduced the task complexity by 90%.”

Review note · Design Decisions pageOpen in Figma ↗ (opens in a new tab)

Changed after review

The checkpoint lost its heatmap

Heat maps didn’t show any value; the price became very vague. Showing exact value against the slot in a tabular format, on the other hand, was very easy to perceive.

“All set” got specific

A synced wallbox screen was not so informative. The earlier effort seemed useless without an acknowledgment of what exactly was fed to the system.

Handed over as a system: 14 color variables, 12 text styles, 17 product components

  2. BProduct components
