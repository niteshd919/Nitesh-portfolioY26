      Stage 00 / 08The brief

      Search is where users tell you exactly what they want.

      Question Why do so many apps still get it wrong, and what would getting it right take?

      It is the one place in an app where people say, in their own words, what they came for. It is also where most apps let them down.

      I set out to find out why, and to turn the evidence into a playbook a product team can build from. It starts at a plain search box and ends with an AI assistant that answers back, across ecommerce, fintech, healthtech and the car.

      Every number on this page names its source. The full reasoning, decision log and confidence ratings live in the research document.

      01Why it mattersFundamentals

      0450m elevation

      Stage 01 / 08Fundamentals

      The first query decides the outcome.

      Question Why does search deserve this much attention?

      Almost half of users whose first search failed gave up immediately.

      Most people won’t rephrase. Each retry cuts the odds of success, and many never make a second attempt. So everything that follows is about one thing: winning the first query by understanding the user’s words, not the app’s.

      Source: Nielsen Norman Group, ecommerce search testing (“Search: Visible and Simple”)

      Search is the highest-intent path in an app

      ~50%
      of shoppers choose search first to find products
      92%
      buy the item after a successful search
      81%
      are likelier to buy elsewhere after a failed search
      78%
      feel less loyal when things are hard to find

      Sources: Baymard Institute 2026; Google Cloud / Harris Poll 2023 (US shoppers)

      Users rely on search for two jobs
      01ControlGoing straight to what they want. 02RescueAn escape hatch when navigation fails.

      Source: Nielsen Norman Group

      02What users expectFundamentals

      0850m elevation

      Stage 02 / 08Fundamentals

      Users bring web-search habits to every app.

      Question What does someone expect the moment they tap the search box?

      Six expectations travel with people from web search engines into every app they open. None of them is negotiable, and each one is measurable.

      1. Expectation 01

        Understand my words

        Synonyms, typos, units and symbols all work.

        54% of stores fail on queries like “13in”

      2. Expectation 02

        Short queries work

        Two words, no syntax.

        2.0 words: the mean query in NN/g’s testing

      3. Expectation 03

        Right first time

        The best answers sit on the first screen. Few users page past it or rephrase.

      4. Expectation 04

        Search everything

        Help, policies, settings and actions too, not only products.

        34% of test users searched for non-product content

      5. Expectation 05

        Feel instant

        Suggestions within about 0.1 s, results within about 1 s, visible progress beyond that.

        0.1 s · suggest1 s · results

      6. Expectation 06

        Never leave me stuck

        Zero results still offers a next step. Generic “search tips” go unread.

      Sources: Baymard Institute 2025–2026; NN/g (2001, 1993)

      03The anatomyPatterns

      1250m elevation

      Stage 03 / 08Patterns

      Every path ends at the goal or a recovery.

      Question What is a search experience actually made of?

      Two components decide most searches

      Autosuggest

      19%

      of stores get every detail right, though 80% offer it

      • Up to about 10 suggestions on desktop, 4–8 on mobile
      • Bold the predicted text, not what the user typed
      • Arrow keys move through and copy into the box
      • Style scope suggestions (“in Laptops”) differently
      • Dim the page; no scrollbar inside the list
      Zero results

      ~50%

      of stores leave users at a dead end

      1. Recovery 01Related categories
      2. Recovery 02Alternative queries, auto-applied if there is only one
      3. Recovery 03Recently viewed items
      4. Recovery 04Help, chat or phone
      5. Recovery 05Popular items

      Search tips alone don’t work: users rarely read them.

      Source: Baymard Institute, autocomplete (2022) and “No Results” page (2025) research · Mock-ups are illustrative

      04Words firstPatterns

      1650m elevation

      Stage 04 / 08Patterns · Ecommerce

      Most failures are vocabulary failures.

      Question Where exactly does search break?

      The pattern

      The further a query drifts from the product’s own name, the more often search fails.

      Exact names fail on only 12% of sites. Help searches like “return policy” fail on two thirds. Overall, 56% of sites fall short, and apps fare worst.

      RuleFix vocabulary before polishing the interface.

      Source: Baymard Institute, Ecommerce Search UX 2026 benchmark (10,000+ ratings)

      Handle all eight ways shoppers search

      Each card is one query type, a query a shopper typed in testing, and what search should do with it. They climb from the easiest to the hardest for today’s stores.

      1. 01

        Exact

        Fails on 12%

        Keurig K45 Elite

        Match titles, SKUs, models and misspellings

      2. 02

        Product type

        Fails on 20%

        sandals

        Jump to the matching category and its filters

      3. 03

        Symptom

        Fails on 37%

        knee pain

        Map problems to the products that solve them

      4. 04

        Feature

        Fails on 39%

        blue shirt

        Pre-apply attributes as visible, removable filters

      5. 05

        Use case

        Fails on 43%

        spring jacket

        Tag products by season, place and occasion

      6. 06

        Compatibility

        Fails on 44%

        Dell XPS 13 charger

        Know what the user owns; show fit on results

      7. 07

        Abbreviation

        Fails on 54%

        13in, tv

        Map short forms and symbols to full forms

      8. 08

        Non-product

        Fails on 66%

        return policy

        Index help and policies; answer directly

      Ranking

      Let margin, stock and promotions only break ties between equally relevant items, and label sponsored results clearly. In a 70-person usability study, sponsored results were noticed but skipped for low trust.

      Sources: Baymard Institute 2026; Search Engine Journal (Indig, 2025) on sponsored results

      05Higher stakesDomains

      2050m elevation

      Stage 05 / 08Domains

      Same search loop, different stakes.

      Question How does search change when a wrong result costs money, health or safety?

      Playbook · Fintech

      Find the one transaction, safely

      One transaction, payee, action or answer, and a miss costs money or privacy. Search here is about precision and trust.

      F

      Find

      • Clean merchant names and logos, not raw bank strings
      • Search merchant, person, notes, amount, place and category across all accounts
      • Numbers as numbers: “1,299”, “₹1,299.00”, “over 5000”
      • Plain dates like “last month”, shown as a filter
      A

      Act

      • Make actions findable: “freeze card”, “download statement”
      • Deep-link to them, but keep step-up authentication
      • Never execute anything from a suggestion
      • Answer fees and limits from the official source, with its date
      P

      Protect

      • Mask account and card numbers in suggestions and recents
      • Let users clear history; hide sensitive recents on shared screens
      • Keep queries out of third-party analytics and URLs

      Confidence: low to medium. Public research on fintech search is thin; validate with your logs and a diary study.

      Sources: Visa Developer; Revolut (2018); Monzo community; Search Engine Journal (Indig, 2025) · Mock-up is illustrative

      Playbook · Healthtech

      Bridge lay words and clinical terms

      The core health search problem is vocabulary. Patients type the left; records and expert content say the right.

      • “dry mouth”xerostomia
      • “flu”influenza
      • “flat head”plagiocephaly

      Sources: Vydiswaran et al., AMIA 2014; Plovnick and Zeng, JMIR 2004

      Two audiences need two designs

      01

      Patients and caregivers

      • Search by symptom or plain name: “skin doctor” finds dermatology
      • Safety first: red-flag queries surface urgent-care guidance
      • Show author, reviewer and review date on every answer
      • Plain-language titles with the clinical term alongside
      02

      Clinicians in the EHR

      • Role-aware results: pharmacists search mostly for medications
      • Suggest synonyms: 33 clinicians rated search higher with them on
      • Show strength, form and route so look-alike drugs can’t be confused
      • Treat repeated searches as a sign information is hard to find

      Keep health queries first-party: no ad or analytics pixels, de-identified logs. US HHS guidance still covers logged-in pages after a 2024 court ruling narrowed it.

      Sources: Kaiser Permanente DOR 2020; Hanauer et al. 2017; Search Engine Journal 2025; Morrison Foerster 2024. Red-flag routing is a design recommendation to define with clinical governance.

      Playbook · Other domains

      Every domain has its own must-have

      The same loop applies everywhere; what changes is the thing being found and the one behaviour search can’t do without.

      DomainWhat users look forMust-have behaviour
      SaaS and B2B toolsRecords, documents, people, commandsCommand palette for actions; permission-aware results
      EdtechConcepts, lessons, video momentsConcept synonyms and misconceptions; transcript search
      Media and streamingTitles, people, moodsHeavy typo tolerance; availability by plan and region
      TravelPlaces, dates, constraintsDisambiguate places (“Paris, TX”); flexible dates
      In-vehicleDestinations, media, settingsVoice first; each glance 2 s or less, 12 s per task (NHTSA)
      IntranetPolicies, people, documentsSearch across tools; best bets for top queries; access control
      In focus · In-vehicle

      In the car, search is safety-critical.

      NHTSA’s voluntary guidelines keep each glance to two seconds and a whole task to twelve seconds of eyes off the road. So in-vehicle search leans on voice and prediction instead of typing.

      Sources: research document synthesis; US DOT / NHTSA visual-manual distraction guidelines

      06Search that talksFrontier

      2450m elevation

      Stage 06 / 08Frontier · AI & conversation

      AI should sit on top of real results.

      Question What changes when search learns to answer back?

      Trust splits by stakes
      Low stakes
      Quick facts often end in the AI summary.
      Money and health
      Users verify with official sources such as PayPal or Mayo Clinic.

      When a summary is wrong or generic, users abandon it.

      Use AI for

      • Turning long queries into editable filters
      • Summarising help content, with citations and dates
      • Hybrid retrieval: keywords for exact strings, vectors for meaning

      Never for

      • Account facts: balances, statuses, test results
      • Health triage without clinical protocols
      • Actions without a confirmation step

      Sources: Search Engine Journal (Indig, 2025; 70-user study); Manticore Search hybrid search manual

      In a chat, search changes shape

      When search lives inside an assistant, the same loop still exists. Each component just changes form.

      ComponentClassic searchIn a chat
      EntryA search boxA prompt box plus starter prompts
      SuggestionsAutocomplete as you typeSuggested follow-ups after each answer
      UnderstandingKeywords, synonyms, filtersIntent and constraints, remembered across turns
      ClarifyingFilters the user picksOne good question, only when it adds value
      ResultsA ranked listAn answer plus cards: products, transactions, doctors
      Showing its workVisible filter chipsThe constraints in use, shown and editable
      RecoveryA zero-results pageRelax constraints, suggest options, reach a human

      Same loop, new shape: the assistant still has to show what it understood, and never leave the user stuck.

      Chat is search by another name

      ~75%
      of chatbot conversations were information-seeking
      3.8%
      included a clarifying question, though users wanted them
      22%
      were followed by any check of the answer

      From a two-week diary study: 425 conversations by 18 people using ChatGPT, Bard and Bing Chat.

      Six conversation types: spot which one is happening, and adapt
      1. Search query

        One simple prompt, no follow-up

      2. Funneling

        Starts vague, narrows step by step

      3. Exploring

        Starts broad, goes deeper to learn

      4. Chiseling

        Covers many facets of one topic

      5. Expanding

        Broadens after a weak answer

      6. Pinpointing

        One detailed, specific prompt

      Ask well or not at all: good clarifying questions raised success and satisfaction; poor ones lowered them.

      Sources: NN/g diary study and conversation types (2023); Zou et al., ACM TOIS (2023)

      Conversation · Ecommerce

      Turn a shopping need into a shortlist

      300M
      customers used Amazon’s Rufus assistant in 2025
      ~$12B
      in extra annualised sales Amazon credits to it
      • Turn the need into constraints, shown as editable chips
      • Ground every pick in the live catalogue: price, stock, specs
      • Ask one useful question when the need is broad
      • Compare in a table when asked “which is better?”
      • Label sponsored picks; ads never pose as advice
      • Hand off to the product page or cart in one tap

      Sources: Amazon Q4 2025 results via Modern Retail (2026), Amazon’s own figures; Baymard (2026) · Chat is illustrative

      Conversation · Fintech

      Answer from records, escalate disputes

      37%
      of the US population used a bank’s chatbot in 2022
      3B+
      interactions with Bank of America’s Erica since 2018
      • Answer from account records, shown as data, not prose
      • Spot disputes and fraud; route them to the formal process
      • Always offer a human; break loops of unhelpful answers
      • Confirm every action and keep step-up authentication

      Say it’s AI. EU chatbots must disclose this from 2 August 2026. RBI’s FREE-AI report (2025) proposes AI-use disclosure and a way to contest AI decisions.

      Sources: CFPB 2023; ATM Marketplace 2025; Cooley 2026; Legal500 2025 · Chat is illustrative

      Conversation · Healthtech

      Ask for the details people leave out

      Why it failed
      • People left out key details
      • Small rewordings changed the answer
      • Good and bad advice came mixed together
      Design the conversation to close the gap
      • Ask for what people omit: age, duration, severity
      • Check red flags first; route to urgent care
      • Same facts, same answer: use vetted, dated content
      • Show what it assumed, so the user can correct it
      • Offer next steps, never a diagnosis
      • For clinicians: answer from the chart and cite the note or lab
      • Guard against over-trust: prompt a check with a clinician

      Sources: Bean et al., Nature Medicine (2026), a randomised study led by Oxford; WHO guidance on large multi-modal models (2024)

      Each domain needs its own conversation

      The same conversational principles, tuned per domain. These rows are design recommendations rather than measured findings.

      DomainUsers askMust-have behaviour
      SaaS and B2B tools“How do I export invoices?”Answer from docs; do tasks only with a preview
      Edtech“Is my answer right?”Give hints and questions before answers
      Media and streaming“Something like X, but shorter”Moods become filters; only titles the user can watch
      Travel“Beach trip in May, under ₹60k”Remember constraints; live prices, never invented
      In-vehicle“Find a charger on my route”Voice first, short replies, confirm before acting
      Intranet“What’s our leave policy?”Answer only from documents the person may see

      In every domain: say it’s AI, cite sources, and keep a path to the classic results and to a human.

      Design synthesis from the research; NHTSA glance limits apply to in-vehicle voice and screens

      07Measure & buildMastery

      2800m elevation

      Stage 07 / 08Mastery

      Measure a stack, then build behind gates.

      Question How do we know it works, and in what order do we build it?

      Measure a stack, not a single number

      Task success is the goal; each layer below explains why it moves. When success drops, read down the stack.

      Pair every metric with a guardrail: cutting zero results by returning everything destroys precision. Segment by query type, and set targets from your own baseline rather than vendor benchmarks.

      Sources: NN/g response-time limits; SearchStax site-search analytics definitions

      1. OutcomeDid users get what they came for?

        Task success · Time to success · Ease rating

      2. BehaviourWhere does the loop break?

        Zero results · Clicks · Exits · Reformulations · Recovery

      3. RelevanceIs ranking getting better?

        nDCG@10 · Precision@k on a judged query set

      4. SystemIs it fast and complete?

        Latency (≈0.1 s suggest, ≈1 s results) · Coverage

      For conversations: measure resolution, not replies

      Classic metrics still apply underneath, but a conversation is judged by whether the task got resolved.

      Outcome: did it solve the task?

      • Resolution rate: solved without a human
      • Escalation quality: reached a human when needed
      • Time to resolution

      Conversation: was it efficient?

      • Turns to resolution
      • Clarifying questions rated useful
      • Repeat-question rate, a sign of loops

      Answer quality: was it right?

      • Citation coverage of claims
      • Unsupported-claim rate on a judged set
      • Safety-routing accuracy

      Trust: was it believed, rightly?

      • Source and verification clicks
      • Thumbs-down rate with reasons
      • Fallback to classic results

      Guardrail: a high containment rate can mean users are trapped. Pair it with escalation quality and the repeat-question rate.

      Sources: CFPB on chatbot loops (2023); Zou et al. (2023); NN/g diary study (2023)

      Build in four phases, each gated by a metric

      Instrument first so every later change can be judged. Fix vocabulary and recovery next, because that is where most failures are. Then the interface, and AI last.

      1. Phase 1

        Instrument

        • Log every search and its outcome
        • Build a judged query set
        • Run a baseline usability test

        GateA dashboard the team trusts

      2. Phase 2

        Vocabulary

        • Synonyms, typos and units from the logs
        • Index help, policies and actions
        • Rebuild the zero-results state

        GateZero results down, precision holds

      3. Phase 3

        Interface

        • Autosuggest to the research pattern
        • Filters, scope and snippets
        • Accessible combobox; A/B tests

        GateTask success up, latency in budget

      4. Phase 4

        Intelligence

        • Hybrid retrieval
        • Learning to rank from behaviour
        • Cited AI answers

        GateRelevance up, no unsupported claims

      Throughout: give search an owner, and review the top zero-result queries, top exits and new terms every week. Phases can overlap, but each gate must hold before the next phase counts as a success.

      08SummitFirst light

      3000m elevation

      Stage 08 / 08Summit

      Where to start on Monday.

      Question With all of this in hand, what are the first three moves?

      1. 1

        Baseline first

        Log every search with results, clicks, exits and reformulations. Pull the top 100 queries and build a judged set.

      2. 2

        Fix words and dead ends

        Add synonyms, units and abbreviations from the logs, index help and actions, and give every zero-result state a next step.

      3. 3

        Agree the ground rules

        Pick the first product, name an owner for search, and confirm which privacy rules govern query logs, such as HIPAA or India’s DPDP Act.

      Then the interface, then AI, each behind its metric gate.

      Win the first query. Never leave anyone stuck. Let AI stand on results people can check.
